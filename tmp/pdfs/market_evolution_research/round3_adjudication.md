# Round 3 Independent Adjudication

Date: 2026-08-05

## Scope and adjudicative standard

This adjudication reviews `Odylith_Market_Evolution_Situational_Awareness.md` and all six Round 2 records: enterprise authority, incumbents, market economics, mechanism design, superintelligence resistance, and antifragility, falsification, and real options. The last record is treated as part of the final evidence set, including its existential kill tree, A01-A25 falsification register, M01-M20 mechanism audit, C01-C22 kill and revival cards, and option-decay tests.

The standard here is non-prescriptive. The question is not what Odylith should build, whether a company should exist, or whether an investor should act. It is whether the dossier has produced a sufficiently unanchored and causally discriminating world model. The tests are: internal consistency; independence from the originating thesis; separation of function, institution, market, and company; explicit treatment of null worlds; evidence proportionality; and conditional rather than rhetorical opportunity claims.

Source locators below name exact dossier headings and, where useful, exact claims. Round 2 locators name the corresponding memo and debate. These citations are internal evidence references, not endorsements of the external sources cited inside those records.

## Executive determination

The dossier is materially stronger than the prior strategy frame it criticizes. It explicitly states in `# Mandate: build a world model before choosing a strategy` that it does not assume a router, control plane, marketplace, clearinghouse, verification layer, governance company, software beachhead, or outcome business must exist. It correctly says in `# Present topology: hierarchy before market` that internal allocation is hierarchy and in `# Open standards: interoperability is not contestability` that message compatibility does not establish independent supply, portable authority, settlement, or remedies. The Round 2 mechanism memo supplies the strongest discipline in the corpus: independent principals, funded demand, executable allocation, liability, payment, settlement, default, remedies, and contestability are required before market language is earned.

Those are real corrections, not cosmetic ones. The dossier also distinguishes marginal inference cost from total system expenditure, nominal provider plurality from customer-control concentration, technical verification from institutional acceptance, resilience from antifragility, and a persistent function from a separately monetizable company. Its superintelligence worlds include genuine death conditions for routing, external assurance, human review, and rich control layers.

The adverse finding is that the synthesis has not fully escaped the originating frame. Its disclaimers are less anchored than its taxonomy. The title is still `Odylith Market Evolution Situational Awareness`; the first ten entries of the `# Opportunity atlas: conditional surfaces, not selections` map closely onto an agent control-plane decomposition; and many later entries are the institutional complements that such a decomposition would need. The document says the deck is only a hypothesis generator, but the generated opportunity inventory still resembles a broadened perimeter around that deck. This is not proof that any category is wrong.

The antifragility memo supplies useful counterevidence to a claim of simple deck copying: its `## Post-hoc collision with the supplied deck` says its analysis was derived before reviewing the deck, and its independently generated C01-C22 functions overlap materially with the dossier. That overlap raises confidence that several functions are real analytical objects. It does not prove complete frame independence because the memo's starting scope already asks whether an independent economic position can exist around routing, context, verification, authorization, governance, evidence, reputation, procurement, settlement, insurance, and trust. It is blind to the deck but not blind to an Odylith-adjacent problem statement. A genuinely independent second taxonomy must begin from actors, welfare, loss allocation, public goods, labor, households, coercion, and non-market institutions rather than from candidate trust-infrastructure functions.

The second adverse finding is that governance and authority receive many death-world qualifications but remain the dossier's default residual scarcities. The synthesis repeatedly moves value from cognition to context, credentials, proof, authority, legitimacy, physical resources, or consent. It does acknowledge that each can die, yet it does not model enough worlds in which enforceable governance is absent, irrelevant, captured, non-commercial, or unable to keep pace. The phrase in `# What superintelligence resistance actually means` that some mechanisms may remain costly to bypass is properly conditional; the opportunity atlas often turns the same conditional functions back into named economic surfaces before separate budget, supplier access, and rent durability have been shown.

The third finding is more favorable: the dossier generally does not confuse hierarchy with market in its definitions, but it sometimes lets market-shaped opportunity language leak back into scenarios where only procurement, internal allocation, or a platform supply chain exists. The mechanism memo corrects this more rigorously than the main synthesis. The main dossier should import that stricter vocabulary everywhere, not only in `# When agent activity becomes a market`.

The fourth finding is that the dossier explicitly rejects standards-as-contestability, but it has not fully operationalized the rejection. `O24. Protocol infrastructure` still assigns scarcity to "Common syntax and network effects." Common syntax may be a public good with no appropriable rent, and network effects can accrue to the dominant runtime, registry, identity root, or distribution platform rather than to protocol infrastructure. The correction must distinguish interoperability, portability, multi-homing, demand control, governance control, and economic contestability as separate variables.

## Agreements that survive cross-examination

### 1. Cheap intelligence and concentrated control can coexist

All six Round 2 records support the dossier's rejection of a one-dimensional abundance story. The market-economics memo shows that falling accepted-task cost can coexist with frontier fixed costs, committed spend, data gravity, and platform bundling. The incumbent memo shows that open or cheap model supply can still sit behind a few identity, context, billing, and runtime owners. The superintelligence memo makes the same point through `O1. Cheap inference, dominant provider` and `O13. Open weights, centralized power`. The antifragility memo's existential kill tree adds the strongest commercial null: cheap reliable intelligence, converged protocols, customer-owned policy, suite bundling, incumbent settlement, accredited conformity, and incumbent risk capital can jointly leave no necessary independent control point.

The dossier's `# Causal loop 1: cheap intelligence, rebound, and total spend` is therefore directionally sound. Its strongest claim is not that rebound must occur, but that falling unit cost and rising aggregate expenditure are compatible. The explicit falsifier - mature cohorts showing saturated task volume, falling total risk-adjusted spend, and no complementary migration - is appropriate. A required caution remains: the current co-movement of efficiency, capex, and electricity does not by itself identify price-induced rebound. Demand anticipation, competitive overbuild, training loads, and non-AI demand can produce similar observations.

### 2. Provider plurality and platform concentration are orthogonal

The dossier and Round 2 agree that counting model names is a poor concentration measure. `# Provider evolution: capability, distribution, and enclosure` correctly locates control in contracts, identity, authoritative context, telemetry, billing, defaults, and committed spend. Round 2 market economics states the tie-break directly: the key question is who owns qualified demand, authoritative context, and the right to act. Round 2 incumbents adds that multiple enclosed systems of record can coexist without creating a neutral cross-system authority.

This agreement should be retained. Dependency-adjusted concentration is more probative than vendor counts, downloads, or protocol membership. The dossier also correctly notes that acquisition can validate a function while disproving an independent category.

### 3. Governance need does not imply governance company

The corpus repeatedly reaches the same important distinction. `# Opportunities strengthened by greater intelligence` says more consequential action can increase demand for constraints, but the function may be absorbed by a trusted provider or state. `# Opportunities that disappear as intelligence improves` says technical redundancy is not required for company death because bundling, internalization, regulation, or systems-of-record control can erase an independent budget. Round 2 market economics sharpens the point: institutional authority may remain scarce while governance software becomes plumbing.

This is one of the dossier's most valuable conclusions. It must, however, become a formal gate on every opportunity entry rather than remain a warning around the atlas.

### 4. Machine activity is not market formation

`# Present topology: hierarchy before market`, `# When agent activity becomes a market`, and `# Machine commerce: a contingent sequence` agree with the Round 2 mechanism memo. A million internal routing decisions can remain one firm's hierarchy. A platform that assembles components behind one customer contract is vertical production. A directory or protocol can lower discovery cost without creating executable exchange. A clearing layer cannot manufacture underlying competitive obligations.

The mechanism memo's staged test is the best-developed causal instrument in the source set. The first plausible market transition is a funded principal issuing a binding RFQ to ownership-independent suppliers under acceptance, liability, and payment terms. Even that is a thin procurement market, not proof of continuous liquidity or autonomous machine commerce. This distinction should govern all market claims in the dossier.

### 5. Verification shifts rather than settles the control point

The enterprise-authority, incumbent, market-economics, mechanism, and superintelligence records all distinguish technical verification from accepted authority. Perfect proof can commoditize output checking while increasing the power of specification authors, key custodians, trust roots, courts, or platforms that define recognized evidence. Provider-native telemetry can be the most complete technical record and still be conflicted or institutionally insufficient. Independent evidence can be required and still reinforce concentration if only large providers can fund access and compliance.

The dossier's S11 claim that proof does not create consent or legal mandate therefore survives. So does the opposite warning in S13 and the superintelligence memo's `O16. Self-verifying outputs, strategically deceptive process`: a perfect proof can validate the selected objective while the process manipulates which objective or facts enter scope.

### 6. Resistance, resilience, bargaining power, and antifragility are distinct

`# What superintelligence resistance actually means` and `# Antifragility under model and institutional shocks` correctly reject redundancy as proof of antifragility. The useful test is whether a bounded shock creates more credible routes, better-calibrated limits, lower switching friction, or greater bargaining power. The source set also correctly identifies fake diversification: multiple logos over a common cloud, insurance without claims capacity, open syntax with proprietary identity, or human oversight without time and authority.

The dedicated antifragility record makes this agreement substantially stronger. Its strict chain is `shock -> protected downside -> captured learning or exercise right -> measurable gain -> retained rights -> bounded failure`. It finds that most supposed antifragile mechanisms are actually resistance, resilience, bargaining power, or learning. Its most credible conditional positive-convexity candidates are incident replay, reusable control mappings, repeated switching maps, and actuarial learning, and each dies when rights disappear, labels are disputed, events correlate, native suites retain observations, or marginal maintenance scales with shock diversity. The dossier should import this stricter test rather than summarize antifragility as a list of generally attractive properties.

## Hidden shared assumptions

### Institutions remain coherent enough to matter

Even skeptical worlds usually preserve recognizable enterprises, states, courts, insurers, procurement offices, property rights, and budgets. S16 and S17 weaken them, but the dossier mostly treats institutional delay, capture, or emergency hierarchy as a transition to another authority holder. A missing null is institutional incoherence: fragmented enforcement, conflicting jurisdictions, bankrupt counterparties, persistent informal economies, or a state that cannot compel the actor controlling infrastructure. In such worlds, governance is neither a durable scarcity nor a bundled feature. It is an unenforced claim.

### Authority is identifiable and attributable

The dossier often asks who controls authority, which presumes that authority can be scoped, recorded, and attributed. Round 2 enterprise authority correctly distinguishes authentication from actual authority, consent, fiduciary duty, and benefit. The synthesis should go further: principals may be ambiguous, mandates may be contested after the fact, coalitions may disagree internally, and autonomous systems may split acts across thresholds or jurisdictions. "Who authorized this?" may have no stable answer even when logs are perfect.

### Economic demand remains human-welfare-linked

The accepted-outcome denominator assumes an identifiable buyer objective. The dossier does not sufficiently model synthetic demand: agents generating tasks, transactions, content, reputation, and defensive responses for other agents without increasing human welfare. Such an economy can display rising calls, payments, energy, market depth, and even accounting revenue while destroying real surplus. The internal-GMV and retry-demand confounders are useful but too narrow. The missing variable is welfare-adjusted final demand.

### Scarcity migrates rather than demand disappearing

`# Causal loop 2: scarcity migration is not guaranteed` verbally rejects guaranteed migration, but much of the document still searches for the next bottleneck. A different world has no compensating scarcity with a commercial budget: intelligence reduces transaction and coordination costs, organizations capture little incremental revenue, prices fall, and aggregate profits compress. Another has productivity gains that become leisure, public benefit, lower prices, or producer surplus captured by existing asset owners. The analytical endpoint need not be a new paid layer.

### Organizations can absorb and measure change

The dossier includes organizational absorption as a state variable and recognizes that time saved need not become P&L. Yet most opportunity and budget paths assume a buyer can define accepted outcomes, reallocate labor, measure full cost, and maintain a coalition long enough to procure a solution. Organizational politics may make measurement impossible, not merely slow. A function can be valuable, observable in a trial, and permanently unfunded because no owner can capture the distributed benefit or accept the concentrated liability.

### Counterparties stay solvent and losses remain compensable

Insurance, warranties, disputes, settlement, and clearing all presume losses can be bounded, priced, and remedied. The dossier names correlated and catastrophic failures but underweights judgment-proof actors, cross-border non-enforcement, state immunity, provider bankruptcy, and harms that money cannot reverse. The existence of evidence and liability does not ensure compensation.

### More information improves decisions

The leading-indicator and antifragility systems assume that evidence can update policy before the regime changes. Under strategic deception, Goodhart effects, political suppression, or fast takeoff, added telemetry may improve the actor's concealment, overwhelm decision makers, or arrive after the option expires. Learning rate relative to environmental change is more important than information volume.

### The firm and state remain the principal units

The source set under-models households, worker organizations, cooperatives, open-source communities, criminal networks, informal institutions, and transnational civil society. This biases authority toward CIOs, CISOs, regulators, providers, and insurers. It also biases opportunities toward enterprise procurement even when value or harm is dispersed across people who do not control the budget.

## Missing-world additions

The following worlds should be added before the scenario set can claim broad situational coverage. They are not forecasts and need no assigned probabilities.

### M1. AI investment bust and capability plateau

Capability improvements slow, enterprise value remains mostly individual augmentation, and overbuilt infrastructure is written down. Models remain useful but no broad autonomous economy forms. Seats consolidate into ordinary software, agent operations remain niche, and assurance budgets shrink with action scope. This world differs from slow takeoff because the binding event is disappointed economics, not merely gradual progress. It directly tests `# Causal loop 1`, the budget-migration path, and most of the opportunity atlas.

### M2. Public-good and internalization equilibrium

Important controls exist but are supplied through open source, regulation, internal enterprise teams, professional norms, or public infrastructure. There is no durable independent vendor category. This world is required because the dossier repeatedly distinguishes function from company but does not give the no-company equilibrium equal scenario weight. It would absorb policy, identity formats, conformance, evidence schemas, and some verification without denying their importance.

### M3. Labor and democratic veto

Workers, professions, consumers, and voters constrain deployment despite favorable benchmark economics. Bargaining, licensing, collective action, due-process claims, or distributional conflict make certain substitutions illegitimate. The binding scarcity is not generic "institutional trust" but consent by groups that bear concentrated harm and may not control the purchasing budget. This world can produce moratoria, mandated human roles, public ownership, or redistribution without a technical catastrophe.

### M4. Synthetic-demand and Goodhart economy

Agents create requests, content, payments, reviews, security events, and verification work for other agents. Reported activity and revenue rise while human welfare, productivity, or fiscal capacity stagnates. Platforms benefit from metered circular activity; enterprises struggle to distinguish necessary work from machine-generated overhead. Market liquidity metrics can look healthy even after wash activity is removed because the underlying objectives themselves are misaligned. The key denominator becomes welfare-adjusted terminal outcome, not accepted machine task.

### M5. Informal, criminal, and adversarial agent economy

Autonomous activity scales outside compliant identity, audit, payment, and procurement rails. Stolen credentials, coercion, unlicensed models, informal settlement, ransomware, evasion, and jurisdiction shopping become material. Governance demand may rise for compliant actors while effective authority shrinks system-wide. This world attacks the assumption that institutional control expands with consequence and that machine commerce becomes more legible as it matures.

### M6. Institutional fracture and non-compensable loss

Courts conflict, states impose capital controls, providers or insurers fail, and harms cross borders faster than judgments. Evidence may be perfect while remedy is unavailable. Liability attaches to insolvent shells or parties outside reach. Clearing and insurance cannot stabilize the system because loss exceeds capital or finality is politically reversed. This is distinct from S18's orderly polycentric mosaic: fragmentation here destroys reciprocal recognition.

### M7. Machine legal standing and autonomous asset control

Law recognizes machine-managed entities, autonomous trusts, or direct machine capacity to hold assets and obligations. Human principals become remote or optional. The dossier's principal-based delegation model must then be rebuilt around constitutional limits, capitalization, revocation, and dissolution rather than attribution to a human click. This world directly reverses the claim in S3 that institutions attach authority to human or legal principals understood in the current sense.

### M8. Non-market abundance and political allocation

Digital intelligence becomes abundant enough that prices and software markets contract, while states, communities, or dominant providers allocate access as a public entitlement, constitutional service, or rationed utility. The key conflict is distribution, not monetization. This world differs from S9 because trusted intelligence need not belong to one provider and from S8 because scarce rights may be resolved politically rather than traded.

### M9. Catastrophic infrastructure regression

War, grid failure, cyber conflict, climate disaster, or supply-chain rupture reduces compute, communications, and institutional capacity. Advanced systems persist unevenly, but the dominant task is degraded operation rather than acceleration. Offline tools, manual skills, and local energy matter; markets and governance may simplify or disappear. This world tests whether the dossier's option set works under shrinking, not expanding, technical capacity.

## Causal gaps and evidence overreach

### Rebound is plausible but not identified

The evidence in `# Evidence baseline as of 5 August 2026` establishes simultaneous efficiency gains and aggregate infrastructure growth. It does not establish that lower inference prices caused the growth or that mature enterprise cohorts will continue expanding accepted tasks. The required correction is to distinguish price elasticity, capability-induced new demand, speculative capacity build, training demand, and substitution from other workloads. The falsifier should also include infrastructure write-downs and persistent low utilization, not only task saturation.

### Bundling evidence establishes attack capability, not enclosure success

The dossier and incumbent memo document that major platforms offer routing, identity, policy, observability, marketplaces, and governance. This proves the ability and incentive to bundle. It does not prove customer adoption, adequacy, retention, or foreclosure. `# Provider evolution: capability, distribution, and enclosure` should separate "feature exists," "feature is default," "feature controls consequential volume," and "feature prevents independent attachment." Without that ladder, acquisition and release activity can be overread as equilibrium evidence.

### Current legal rules are weak evidence under regime change

The enterprise-authority memo uses current natural-person duties, deployer obligations, consumer protections, and automated-contracting rules carefully. The main dossier sometimes carries them farther through scenario language about legal principals, consent, and accountability. Under S16, S17, M6, or M7, these are variables, not anchors. Present law can identify transition friction but cannot support medium-confidence claims that authority or liability remains scarce under superintelligence.

### The scenario set lacks interaction causality

Each of S1-S18 has a trigger, play-by-play, bottleneck, and falsifier, but the scenarios are mostly one-path narratives. They do not show how one state variable changes another's sign. For example, open standards can strengthen hyperscalers only if identity, hosting, and distribution remain concentrated; regulation can open supply only if compliance artifacts are reusable and procurement lots remain accessible; perfect verification can decentralize generation only if specifications and recognition are not captured. The dossier should express these as conditional interactions, not just adjacent prose.

### "Strengthened by greater intelligence" is too coarse

`# Opportunities strengthened by greater intelligence` says broader credentials, irreversible actions, consequence volume, and physical scope can strengthen constraints. Greater capability can also reduce error rates, compress incident frequency, improve native controls, or concentrate delegation in a trusted system. The relevant independent variable is not intelligence level but consequence-adjusted autonomous exposure minus native prevention and reversibility. The heading should be changed or the claim narrowed.

### Opportunity revenue language exceeds available evidence

The repeated statement that a possible revenue mechanism does not establish willingness to pay is good discipline. Still, assigning a payer and meter to every surface creates an unsupported impression of economic completeness. A payer label such as CIO, CISO, regulator, or insurer does not show procurement authority, incremental budget, renewal, or willingness to add a vendor. The atlas needs explicit evidence states and a null result: no separate payment even though the function exists.

### Antifragility is well falsified but not yet empirically established

`# Antifragility under model and institutional shocks` gives a valid high-level definition, and the Round 2 antifragility memo supplies the missing rigor. Its `## Antifragility mechanism audit` classifies most candidates as resistance, resilience, bargaining power, or learning. It identifies only conditional positive-convexity paths and gives each a kill test. That is strong falsification architecture, not empirical proof that agent systems have already gained from shocks. The dossier should therefore import the memo's gain unit, retained-rights test, positive-convexity interval, and bounded-failure condition, while continuing to label the surviving mechanisms as hypotheses. Post-incident spending, a larger log corpus, survival, and restored service are not gains by themselves.

## Opportunity conditionality: required gate structure

The opportunity atlas should not be deleted, ranked, or converted into a recommendation. It should be made more conditional. The Round 2 antifragility memo already demonstrates the right form through its existential kill tree, A01-A25 decisive falsifiers, C01-C22 death and revival conditions, and option abandonment signals. The dossier should incorporate that structure rather than leave it in a separate appendix. Every O1-O25 entry should pass seven separate gates:

1. Functional gate: the function remains necessary in the stated world.
2. Loss gate: a buyer experiences a measurable loss or foregone gain without it.
3. Authority gate: that buyer can change the relevant decision or workflow.
4. Budget gate: a separate, recurring budget exists after native controls are configured.
5. Access gate: an independent supplier receives enough telemetry, enforcement rights, or ground truth to perform.
6. Rent gate: bundling, standards, internalization, and competition do not drive price below sustainable full cost.
7. Time gate: the window lasts longer than procurement, integration, certification, and capital formation.

The dossier currently covers parts of gates 1, 5, 6, and 7 through formation, dependencies, incumbent response, and death conditions. It does not consistently distinguish gates 2 through 4. It also evaluates death conditions mostly one at a time. Correlated death matters: one trusted integrated provider can simultaneously eliminate routing, external policy, independent evidence, portable reputation, and cross-venue settlement. Conversely, institutional fragmentation can strengthen local compliance demand while destroying global scale and evidence portability.

The antifragility memo makes the correlated commercial null explicit: all routes to durable scarcity can close at once even while many functions remain useful. It also shows why a revival condition is not a recommendation. Breaking one terminal assumption may create a control point, but the resulting function still must pass payer, access, rent, and time gates. The dossier should preserve that existential asymmetry: one surviving function is enough to keep a world analytically open, but not enough to establish a company, market size, or investable return.

For market-facing surfaces - O11 machine payments, O12 procurement auctions and RFQs, O13 portable reputation, O14 insurance, O15 dispute resolution, and O16 settlement and clearing - add the mechanism memo's formation verdict. Protocol messages, mandates, quotes, certifications, and payment initiation are complements, not proof of an underlying market. Each entry should report ownership-adjusted counterparties, executable offers, net external settlement, default experience, remedies, and bypass.

For standards-facing surfaces - especially O24 - add a contestability stack: open specification; multiple conforming implementations; portable identity and data; substitutable registries and trust roots; non-proprietary extensions; successful full-workflow switching; distributed traffic and revenue; and enforceable anti-self-preference. Passing the first layer is not evidence of passing the last.

For governance-facing surfaces - O4 through O9, O23, and O25 - add an authority-null test: what happens if provider-native controls are accepted, policy is internalized, legal enforcement lags, human review becomes ceremonial, or the SI/operator becomes the de facto sovereign? A function that survives only as an embedded provider duty is not an independent opportunity surface.

## High-leverage variables

| Variable | Why it dominates several debates | Minimum observable |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Ownership-adjusted control concentration | Breaks the tie between nominal plurality and real outside options across model, cloud, identity, context, verifier, and settlement layers. | Share of consequential workflows movable without losing identity, memory, evidence, or contract economics. |
| Institutionally accepted verification cost | Separates technical proof from evidence that courts, insurers, regulators, and counterparties will act on. | Cost, latency, and dispute outcome for native versus independent evidence by consequence class. |
| Authority reversibility | Determines whether delegation creates manageable exposure or irreversible principal-agent loss. | Time to revoke, rollback, compensate, and restore state after a contested action. |
| Task contractibility and damage latency | Determines whether routing, auctions, outcome contracts, verification, and insurance can form. | Ex ante acceptance completeness plus the share of material loss discovered after settlement. |
| Welfare-adjusted final demand | Distinguishes productive rebound from synthetic workload, retries, defensive traffic, and circular machine activity. | Human or enterprise outcome net of compute, review, externalities, and machine-generated overhead. |
| Organizational capture rate | Determines whether adoption becomes a P&L budget or remains seats, slack, work intensification, or unallocated quality. | Mature-cohort change in output, cost base, demand, and risk after full implementation cost. |
| Capability-change to institution-update ratio | Determines whether law, policy, insurance, and learned controls can remain relevant. | Time for capability or behavior change divided by time for detection, rule change, deployment, and enforcement. |
| Dependency-adjusted option exercisability | Separates real antifragility from multi-logo redundancy. | Successful failover under loss of the shared cloud, identity root, power region, or legal rail. |
| Retained learning rights and convexity | Determines whether shocks create an owned reusable asset or merely impose losses and public learning. | Out-of-sample gain per bounded incident after data-rights, labeling, remediation, and attacker-adaptation costs. |
| Externality incidence and loss-bearing capacity | Determines whether liability, insurance, and market prices discipline behavior. | Who bears realized loss, available capital, exclusions, recovery rate, and non-compensable harm. |
| Standard-stack contestability | Determines whether openness distributes power or lowers complement cost for incumbents. | Switching and traffic concentration across implementation, registry, identity, extension, and settlement layers. |
| Physical actuation coupling | Determines whether digital abundance translates into economic output or remains bounded by machines, energy, land, and safety. | Share of value requiring scarce physical access and the reversibility of physical action. |

These variables should be tracked as interactions. No single threshold resolves the world. For example, cheap accepted verification supports open competition only when task specifications are adequate, trust roots are plural, and full workflows can switch. Otherwise it can strengthen a monopoly specification owner.

## Final boundary

This adjudication does not choose a product, company strategy, financing path, or investment. It finds that the dossier has achieved strong conceptual discipline on hierarchy, concentration, verification, antifragility, and conditionality, but has not yet proven independence from an Odylith-shaped control-plane frame. Its most important next correction is not a new opportunity ranking. It is to expand the null worlds, convert overlapping scenarios into interacting dimensions, force every opportunity through separate function-budget-access-rent-time gates, and make the strict mechanism-design and positive-convexity vocabularies govern the entire document.

The dossier should preserve its central warning: abundant intelligence does not determine who captures value. It should add the equally important warning that value need not migrate to a new commercial layer at all, governance need not remain enforceable, open standards need not create contestability, and machine activity need not become a market.
